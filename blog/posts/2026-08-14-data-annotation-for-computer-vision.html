Data annotation for computer vision: quality over volume

Detection and classification models only learn what the labels teach them. Inconsistent boxes or class names quietly destroy performance.

Before scaling a labeling workforce, lock guidelines: what counts as an object, how to handle occlusion, when to skip frames, and how rare classes are defined. Then add a review loop — even a small sample of dual-annotated images surfaces disagreement early.

Projects such as traffic sign classification and multimodel YOLO experiments depend on this foundation. Volume helps only after quality is under control.

For teams in Pakistan and freelancers taking annotation-heavy contracts, documenting the guideline set is as important as the tooling (CVAT, Label Studio, or custom UIs).
